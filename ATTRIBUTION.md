## Rules-content foundation: SRD 5.1 under CC BY 4.0

The rules/content foundation this project intends to draw on is the
**Dungeons & Dragons System Reference Document 5.1 (SRD 5.1)**, which Wizards of
the Coast released under the **Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International
license (CC BY 4.0)** in January 2023. The CC release is irrevocable.

Authoritative sources:

- SRD 5.1 (Creative Commons PDF): <https://media.dndbeyond.com/compendium-images/srd/5.1/SRD_CC_v5.1.pdf>
- D&D Beyond SRD page (license background/FAQ): <https://dndbeyond.com/srd>
- CC BY 4.0 legal code: <https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode>

The SRD 5.1 "Legal Information" preamble states that any work using SRD 5.1
content must include the following attribution statement. It is reproduced
**verbatim**, extracted with `pdftotext` from the official Creative Commons PDF
(`SRD_CC_v5.1.pdf`, "Legal Information" page) on 2026-10-02:

> This work includes material taken from the System Reference Document 5.1
> ("SRD 5.1") by Wizards of the Coast LLC and available at
> https://dnd.wizards.com/resources/systems-reference-document. The SRD 5.1 is
> licensed under the Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International License
> available at https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/legalcode.

The same preamble adds two constraints:

> Please do not include any other attribution regarding Wizards other than
> that provided above. You may, however, include a statement on your work that
> it is "compatible with fifth edition" or "5E compatible."

This project's required attribution therefore lives in this file and in the
README link below; do **not** invent additional Wizards attribution wording.
**Human legal review is still recommended before public release** — the text
above is verified against the source PDF, but a human should confirm the
presentation context (and whether any OGL 1.0a obligations apply to content
actually used, which the CC path is intended to avoid) before distribution.

## Project-original content

Everything in this repository that is not explicitly marked as SRD-derived —
source code, UI text, original maps, original monsters/items/NPCs, placeholder
artwork, and documentation — is project-original and belongs to the project
authors under the repository's own code license. SRD attribution requirements
do not transfer project-original content into the SRD, and vice versa.

## Third-party assets

Any third-party asset (artwork, fonts, icons, audio, tilesets, sprites,
textures) must carry a license compatible with this project's distribution
model and be recorded with its license and attribution requirements at the
place it is used or in a per-asset manifest. Assets under incompatible or
unclear licenses must not be added.

## Streaming integrations grant nothing

The YouTube and Spotify ambience integrations are **playback links for each
user's own entitled access**. They do **not** grant DnDTable any right to copy,
redistribute, re-host, or bundle third-party audio/video content.

## The SRD is not "all of D&D"

Users and contributors must **not** assume arbitrary D&D material is covered by
the SRD. Notably **not** covered: content from books outside the SRD (e.g. most
of the *Monster Manual* / *Player's Handbook* / *Dungeon Master's Guide* text
and statblocks), adventure books, D&D Beyond catalog content, campaign-setting
fiction/lore (e.g. Forgotten Realms) unless separately licensed, official
artwork and maps, logos and trade dress, and any proprietary music or audio.
The SRD also deliberately renames or omits certain named creatures, items, and
settings; absence from the SRD means no rights are granted.
